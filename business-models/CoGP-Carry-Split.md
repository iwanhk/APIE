# Co-GP with Carry Split

## Definition

Two entities jointly act as general partner of an investment fund and split the economics — most visibly carried interest (a share of profits above a hurdle/high-water mark), and optionally management fees. Co-GP is a **joint-venture structure for fund management**: one party brings assets/strategy, the other brings capital/distribution/education.

## When To Use

- Complementary capabilities: partner GP has the strategy and track record; the co-GP has distribution, client relationships, or an operating platform
- Both sides want genuine alignment (carry only pays out if the fund profits)
- The co-GP is willing to take on fiduciary duties and regulatory obligations (it is no longer a "channel")

## When NOT To Use

- When the co-GP only wants distribution economics — a placement agent or trailing-fee structure is simpler and carries less liability
- When the partner GP controls all decisions and the co-GP has no governance rights — then "co-GP" is a label, not a structure

## The Carry Math (sign conventions: profits positive, carry is a share of profits)

Given: fund assets $10M, carry rate 20%, profits above high-water mark $5M, co-GP share 50%:

| Item | Calculation | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross carry | $5M × 20% | $1.0M |
| Co-GP carry | $1.0M × 50% | $0.5M |
| Partner GP carry | $1.0M × 50% | $0.5M |
| Management fee (if 50/50, 2%/yr) | $10M × 2% × 50% | $0.1M/yr |

Carry is **earned revenue, not recurring revenue**: it pays only after profits and high-water mark recovery, and can be clawed back. A business that depends on carry must have cash flow from somewhere else (management fee share, service fees, or sponsor capital) while the fund is building track record.

**Tiered carry:** many co-GP deals use step-up schedules — e.g., 20% carry on profits up to a threshold, 30% above it (each fund negotiates its own). Model per-fund, never one schedule for the whole platform: a single "carry %" field is a modeling error.

## Governance Essentials

- Investment committee composition and veto rights
- High-water mark, hurdle rate, loss carry-forward mechanics — written, not implied
- Co-investment by both GPs (skin in the game)
- Disclosure: any affiliated platform/marketing must declare the co-GP relationship
- Exit, deadlock, and dissolution terms

## Licensing & Legal Triggers (HK example — jurisdiction-specific)

- Managing a fund from Hong Kong is a Type 9 regulated activity (asset management), regardless of where the fund is domiciled (Cayman/BVI do not remove the obligation)
- Marketing/distributing fund interests to investors may be Type 1 (dealing in securities); offering to professional investors may exempt prospectus authorization but not the actor's licensing duty
- Professional investor verification (e.g., HK individuals: portfolio ≥ HK$8M per the SFO Professional Investor Rules) is a documentary process, not a checkbox
- Tax treatment of carried interest and the fund's transparency status require specialist advice

## Pitfalls

- **Carry ≠ control:** a 50/50 carry split without governance rights is a compensation scheme, not a partnership
- **Cash-flow mismatch:** free distribution platforms funded only by future carry run out of money before the carry exists
- **Affiliation blindness:** the co-GP's own platform recommending its own funds is a conflict unless disclosed and structurally bounded
- **License timing:** education content may launch before licensing, but subscription facilitation cannot — sequence the launch around the license, not the other way around

## Cross-Domain Transfers

- The structure generalizes to any two-sided joint venture where economics and control must be explicitly separated: tech partnerships, content co-productions, and healthcare platform-operator deals
- Reference application: a free education platform for professional investors, monetized through co-GP economics
